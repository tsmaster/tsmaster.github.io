Selected work

Star Wars: Zero Company

Bit ReactorPrincipal AI EngineerMay 2024 – presentWindows, PS5, XboxUnreal Engine

I built AI for enemies in Star Wars: Zero Company, a turn-based tactics game, as one of two AI engineers working with one AI designer. The AI is a one-turn lookahead planner that uses influence-map techniques to score possible move locations and enemy targets. I worked on many of the enemy systems, including:

  • Droideka targeting: Droidekas look for firing positions where they can hit several targets at once, and move there in preference to a single-target shot.
  • Reinforcements: new characters entering the battlefield find good cover on their own as they arrive.
  • Ability targeting: how enemies choose targets for their abilities, worked out with the UI systems lead.

Project Archer: AR digital twins

WalmartSenior Engineer, then Tech LeadMay 2022 – February 2024HoloLens 2, Magic Leap 2Unity

Project Archer was a tech innovation studio within Store No 8, Walmart's tech incubation division. First as a senior engineer and then as tech lead, I built augmented reality digital twin systems in Unity to help employees get work done and customers find great products.

We investigated building "as built" digital twins of real Walmart stores. To line the headsets up with the model, we designed 3D-printed frames for ArUco markers: a Magic Leap 2 could spot a marker, anchor itself to it, and match what it saw to where the digital twin said the shelving units were.

We also evaluated headsets for restocking: an associate could scan inventory barcodes in the back room, and the headset would query Walmart's inventory service to tell whether a box needed to go out to the shelves where customers could reach it.

BattleTech

Harebrained SchemesLead AI EngineerFebruary 2016 – September 2018Windows, macOS, LinuxUnity

I built BattleTech's entire AI system from scratch, as the only AI engineer, working with one AI designer. Enemy squads of four mechs had to fight as a team. The system's three main parts:

  • Behavior trees: the AI's "brain" was described in XML. Python tools turned that description into C# code that ran at full speed inside the Unity project, and drew each tree as a GraphViz flowchart so the designer and I could see exactly how a unit would decide.
  • Influence maps: let units weigh line of sight and facing when deciding where to move.
  • Designer weights: a set of tunable weights let the designer adjust the priority of different behaviors without code changes.

The designer was the system's first customer. He asked for features, and we iterated until the AI played the way he wanted. Near the end of the project, I also built localization support and string interpolation, and worked with the artists on fog of war. I later presented a talk on BattleTech's AI architecture at PAX Dev 2019 (see Talks).

My favorite feedback: coworkers complaining that the AI was a little too good at taking them out with well-timed headshots.

Talks

Show Me Your War Face! The Tactical AI of BattleTech

PAX Dev 2019August 27, 2019Co-presented with Kevin Maloney, BattleTech's AI designer

How do you make a squad of four enemy mechs fight as a team? This talk covered the behavior tree system I built from scratch for BattleTech: trees defined in XML, turned by a Python script into generated C# that was compiled into the game. Influence maps let units weigh line of sight and facing when choosing where to move, and a set of designer-facing weights let the designer adjust the priority of different behaviors.

The Life of a Niantic Map Tile

PAX Dev 2019August 27, 2019

How do you build a game world the size of the real world, and keep it up to date? This talk followed a map tile from start to finish: road and building data taken from OpenStreetMap, sliced into tiles on a latitude/longitude grid, and sent to the game as vector data for roads and building footprints. Game code then styled those shapes and turned them into playable 3D graphics.
